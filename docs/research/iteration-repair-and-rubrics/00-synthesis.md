# Fixing What the AI Missed — Synthesis

**The question, in the owner's words:** *"I have AI build a prototype or feature, I then have to vibe code it a few rounds. Then I test it and x was missed, so it needs a patch. What's the best way to fix that and how are folks handling it now?"*

**The answer, in two moves.**

**1. The repair move.** Do not tell it to fix the thing. Convert the miss into an address and a number, commit, scope-test it on a second route and breakpoint, write the assertion that fails now, *then* pick the move: patch the value, regenerate the unit, or roll back and re-anchor. The patch is the right default and it is right in a clean context — a one-value change with a check that fails before and passes after is the whole job. What makes it go wrong is not the patch, it is the conversation it is issued into: multi-turn performance falls ~39% with unreliability up ~112% ([Laban et al., ICLR 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/llms-get-lost-in-multi-turn-conversation/), [fetched] in [doc 02](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md)), and 40–73% of multi-turn programming tasks lose previously-correct behaviour ([search summary], doc 02 §2). The one stopping rule anybody publishes is the vendor's: **after two failed corrections, stop correcting** — roll back, `/clear`, re-anchor on the file and the failing check, re-prompt once. The twelve-row decision table and the twelve-step patch protocol in [doc 02](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md) are the executable form of this paragraph.

**2. The ratchet.** Then the miss has to stop recurring — and the single most useful finding in the stream is that **the answer is almost never a rubric line.** Of twelve miss classes with evidence behind them, **four** are worth a judged criterion; the other eight are a gate backlog ([doc 01 §8](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md)). A miss a program can decide becomes the program. The rubric is the residue after determinism has taken everything it can take, and for the residue the promotion threshold is the vendor's two-strike rule — add it when the same mistake happens a second time, never the first ([Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory), [fetched]). Fix-and-forget is the correct default and should be *recorded* as a decision so the count exists next time ([doc 03 §2](03-from-miss-to-rubric-line.md)).

**And the part nobody will tell you.** Docs 05 and 06 went looking, independently, for any published measurement that a rules file or a review agent reduces recurrence of a class of miss. **There is none, anywhere, by anyone** — and a GitHub search for tooling that turns corrections into proposed rules returns **zero repositories**. The thing the owner is asking about has no product and no evidence base. That is this stream's central finding, not a caveat: everything above is mechanism reasoning and vendor doctrine, and the cheapest useful thing this repo could do is measure it (E0–E3 below).

## Verdicts — seven falsifiable claims

Each is stated so it can be killed, with the doc that carries the evidence and what would falsify it.

**V1 — The miss set is the complement of the plausibility check.** Everything the generator stops on (it renders, the elements are there, the prose is fluent) is what does not get missed; everything orthogonal to appearance is what does. One sentence in Anthropic's own docs predicts the whole taxonomy. *Evidence:* [doc 01 §1–2](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md), grounded on [best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) [fetched] plus Interaction2Code's ten failure types [fetched] and WebGen-Bench's 27.8% agent-driven functional accuracy [search summary]. *Falsified by:* a defect log where visually-evident defects (spacing, missing elements) survive to the human at the same rate as invisible ones.

**V2 — Iteration is a source term, not only a sink.** Rounds 2..n add misses. ~16–37% of applied patches break pre-existing tests; 66.99% of agent-misalignment episodes are scope overreach in a 20,574-session corpus; 40–73% of multi-turn programming tasks lose previously-correct behaviour. *Evidence:* [doc 01 §3](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md), [doc 02 §2](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md) — **all three figures are [search summary]**, which is why E1 exists. *Falsified by:* a measured regression rate near zero in a design loop with a real baseline.

**V3 — The conversation is the perishable part; the artifact is the durable part.** Effective context length is far below window size; position in context matters more than presence; the documented remedy is amnesia, not persuasion. Every durable thing this stream recommends — spec line, failing test, rules file, defect record — is a bet on externalising state out of the transcript. *Evidence:* [doc 02 §2–3](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md); NoLiMa's per-model effective lengths (GPT-4o 8K, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 4K, Llama 3.3 70B 2K) [fetched from the repo README]. *Falsified by:* fix-forward beating cleared-and-re-anchored on the same defect set (E1).

**V4 — The taxonomy is a gate backlog, not a rubric backlog.** Four of twelve classes earn a judged criterion. A miss a deterministic gate can catch should become the gate; writing it as a rubric line converts a zero-false-positive check into a probabilistic one and spends judge attention forever. *Evidence:* [doc 01 §6, §8](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md); [doc 03 §3](03-from-miss-to-rubric-line.md); [doc 04 §4](04-design-craft-as-checkable-criteria.md), whose 30-row "measurable without judgment" table is the practical proof. *Falsified by:* a class where the deterministic check and the judge disagree with the human in the same direction.

**V5 — Binary for regression, holistic for quality.** Atomic binary criteria raise machine agreement (CheckEval +0.45, [fetched]); binary checklists provably fail to capture expertise in human raters (Hodges 1999: experts scored *worse* than novices on checklists while scoring better on global scales [search summary]). Both are true and they apply to different jobs: a binary criterion answers *did the known failure recur*, a pairwise or holistic judgement answers *is this good*. *Evidence:* [doc 03 §4–5](03-from-miss-to-rubric-line.md). *Falsified by:* the Hodges experiment run on design artifacts without the ranking inverting (E4).

**V6 — The anti-slop corpus is aimed at the wrong half of the problem.** Practitioner rule sets are overwhelmingly visual and string tells — 43 rules in ui-craft, 58 gates in Hallmark, 7 cardinal sins in open-design, all fetched and counted — while the only expert evaluation of generated UI against Nielsen's ten heuristics found the failures concentrated in **help, error recovery, efficiency of use and error prevention** ([CHI 2026 EA](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772363.3799002), [search summary], 138 screens, three tools). A rubric weighted toward visual polish will report improvement while the real gap stays open. *Evidence:* [doc 04 §1–2](04-design-craft-as-checkable-criteria.md). *Falsified by:* verifying the CHI paper and finding the support rates evenly distributed across heuristics.

**V7 — The ratchet is unmeasured, and nobody is selling it.** No published measurement, in any venue, that writing a miss into a rule or a rubric reduces recurrence; zero GitHub repositories for correction→rule generation; no design-defect recall benchmark for any review agent; no design rubric library in any harness; no product that joins the artifact, the defect class, the fix altitude and whether it recurred. *Evidence:* [doc 05 §Tier-3 item 10](05-practitioner-field-guide-x.md), [doc 06 §10, §Gaps](06-community-practice-and-tooling.md) — established independently. *Falsified by:* anyone publishing a before/after recurrence rate. Nobody has.

**V8 — A hand-checked criterion is the only rung that can be permanently net-negative.** A deterministic check costs its build once and occupies zero instruction budget; a prose line costs its neighbours adherence; a criterion you eyeball every round costs you every round, forever, and needs ~1 recurrence/month just to cover its running cost (≈10.5/month if you verify per round rather than per session). Batching verification to session boundaries is worth more than any tool choice. *Evidence:* [doc 07 §1, §3, Deliverable A](07-loop-economics-and-ownership.md) — arithmetic with stated assumptions, not a measurement. *Falsified by:* timing your own `B`, `p` and `D` and getting a different ordering.

## The loop, composed

One pass, from generation to a criterion that retires. The rule column is the load-bearing half.

| # | Step | What happens | The rule | Owner | Doc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Generate** | Generator emits; ledger row records skill SHA, catalog version, model id, prompt hash | The stopping criterion is plausibility — assume the miss set is everything invisible to a look | Designer / pipeline | [01 §1](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md) |
| 2 | **Gate** | Craft floor runs: axe at AA, no overflow at six widths, token/scale adherence, state presence, focus-visible, placeholder grep, motion bounds | No human and no judge sees an artifact a program could have rejected. 20 checks, all deterministic | Automation | [04 §C](04-design-craft-as-checkable-criteria.md), [01 §4](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md) |
| 3 | **Find the miss** | Human click-through, or a design-review agent at three viewports first | The builder tests the route they prompted for; when a miss reaches you, ask *which cheap gate was not running* | Designer | [01 §4](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md), [06 §7](06-community-practice-and-tooling.md) |
| 4 | **Freeze the evidence** | Annotated screenshot, route + state, `getBoundingClientRect`/`getComputedStyle` for element *and parent*, console excerpt | A design defect becomes numbers and an address before any move is chosen. "Make it look better" fails on all four counts | Designer | [02 §5](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md) |
| 5 | **Commit** | `git add -A && git commit`, or `git stash -u`; branch or worktree for anything speculative | Agent checkpoints exclude everything a shell wrote. The only checkpoint you should trust is a commit | Designer | [02 §3](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md) |
| 6 | **Scope-test** | Same element on a second route, a second breakpoint, the opposite theme | Localise element → component → token → rule. Two minutes here routinely turns a patch into a one-line token change | Designer | [02 §5](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md) |
| 7 | **Write the check** | The assertion that fails now and must pass after | If you cannot express "fixed" as something a machine evaluates, stop before patching | Designer | [02 §4](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md), [05 §3.3](05-practitioner-field-guide-x.md) |
| 8 | **Choose the move** | Patch / regenerate the unit / roll back + re-anchor / escalate a level | First matching row of the decision table wins. Two corrections on the same issue = the context is the problem, not the prompt | Designer | [02 §8](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md) |
| 9 | **Repair, bounded** | Minimal-diff prompt: address, evidence, expected value, bounds, check — with a STOP clause | Smallest *correct* diff. ≥3 files or >100 lines is a rewrite wearing a patch's clothes | Agent | [02 §4, §8](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md) |
| 10 | **Verify** | Run the check, the existing suite, and a **component-scoped** diff; review the diff, not the summary | Only the region you touched should differ. A patch may never increase the raw-value count | Designer | [02 §4](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md), [04 §6](04-design-craft-as-checkable-criteria.md) |
| 11 | **Second opinion** | Fresh session, artifact only, never the build transcript | The thing that produced it does not grade it — and the bias is triggered by *where the artifact sits in the conversation*, so the fix is free | Designer | [07 §5.3](07-loop-economics-and-ownership.md), [05 §Recs](05-practitioner-field-guide-x.md) |
| 12 | **Sign out (15 s)** | Each miss gets a class label and one of three decisions: prose criterion now / candidate, tally it / not a class | This is the step the adherence literature predicts you will drop. Make it cost 15 seconds and give it a structural trigger | Rubric owner | [07 §6.1](07-loop-economics-and-ownership.md), [01 §9](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md) |
| 13 | **Promote** | Five tests — recurrence ≥2, class-generality, cost, checkability, discrimination — then the verification ladder: impossible → blocked → linted → asserted → judged → human | A miss a deterministic gate can catch becomes the gate, never a sentence, and never a judge line restating what the gate decides | Rubric owner | [03 §2–3](03-from-miss-to-rubric-line.md) |
| 14 | **Write the criterion** | Atomic, binary, explicit "Fails if…", evidence located by selector/bbox, one pass and one fail exemplar | Three acceptance tests, ten minutes: fail-first on the motivating artifact, pass-clean on the current best, and **flip when negated** | Rubric owner | [03 §4, §6](03-from-miss-to-rubric-line.md) |
| 15 | **Retire and demote** | Weekly: retire at 0 fires in 20 prototypes; demote anything that became computable | The healthiest metric of a rubric is its shrink rate. A rubric that only grows is a tooling failure wearing a governance costume | Rubric owner | [03 §6](03-from-miss-to-rubric-line.md), [07 §9](07-loop-economics-and-ownership.md) |
| 16 | **Measure** | Three integers and a grep per prototype: misses at first human test, rounds to acceptance, repeat-offence rate, canary fired? | Your felt sense is not evidence — the RCT that measured this found a 39-point gap between believed and actual speed | Designer | [07 §7](07-loop-economics-and-ownership.md) |

## The contradictions, carried

Three findings in this stream cut against the obvious advice and against each other. Together they are the stream's strongest thesis, so they are stated before they are resolved.

**(a) Decomposition helps machines and hurts experts.** CheckEval's binary checklists improve evaluator agreement by **+0.45** (README [fetched]) and TICK lifts LLM–human exact agreement 46.4%→52.2% [search summary]. But Hodges et al. 1999 found experienced clinicians scored **significantly worse** than residents and clerks on binary content checklists while scoring significantly better on global process ratings, and Ilgen's review of 45 studies pooled inter-item reliability at **GRS 0.92 vs checklist 0.66** [both search summary]. A checklist measures thoroughness; expertise shows up as *omission*. Applied to UI, a checklist rubric rewards the screen with the tooltip, the breadcrumb, the extra confirmation, and penalises the restrained one. **Resolution:** use atomic binary criteria for *regression* (did this known failure recur — a fact) and pairwise or holistic judgement for *quality* (is this good — a preference). This is not a compromise; it is the repo's existing "rank for improvement, gate for acceptance" invariant ([eval-tuning-loops/00](../eval-tuning-loops/00-synthesis.md)) reached from a second direction. **Left open:** nobody has run the Hodges design on design artifacts. Doc 03 names it "the single highest-value experiment in this document" and it is E4 below. Both Hodges and Ilgen are search summaries; the recommendation rests on them and should not survive their failure to verify.

**(b) An instruction can be negative-valued where a gate is free.** The W4A 2026 study of six AI UI-generation tools reports 29.0% WCAG compliance and — the part that matters — that **explicitly asking for accessibility in the prompt *decreased* compliance**. Meanwhile a rubric used as a reward gets hacked: after rubric-based training, rubric judges preferred the new checkpoint on **85.8%** of prompts while rubric-free judges preferred the base on **78.4%**, with factual correctness, conciseness and relevance all degrading [search summary]. Against both: axe-core is deterministic, claims zero false positives, and covers ~57% of a11y issues by volume ([fetched], Deque's own claim). **Resolution:** the ordering rule is not "gates are better than sentences", it is stronger — *a sentence about a defect class can be worth less than nothing, while a gate on the same class is free after it is built*. That is why 8 of 12 classes get a gate and no criterion, and why the starter rubric's judged lines are all "quality of a thing that is present" — every "does X exist" question has already been taken by a gate. **Left open, loudly:** the W4A result is [search summary] in doc 01 and explicitly **relayed and unverified** in doc 03, which could not locate a primary source before its search budget ran out. It is the most load-bearing unverified claim in the stream. Verify it or drop it.

**(c) The corpus is polishing the half that was already fine.** The practitioner anti-slop corpus is large, converging, and almost entirely visual: purple→cyan gradients, gradient text, emoji feature icons, `transition: all`, twelve-step type ladders, `z-index: 9999`, em-dash floods. Counted from the source files today: ui-craft **43 rules** (14 critical / 25 major / 2 minor / 2 warn), Hallmark **58 gates**, open-design **7 P0 sins** [all fetched and parsed]. Against that, the only expert evaluation of 138 AI-generated mobile screens against Nielsen's ten heuristics found low support concentrated on **H10 help, H9 error recovery, H7 efficiency, H5 error prevention** — "a significant performance gap between visual and functional usability" [search summary]. Doc 04 checked the corpus against this and found **four rules** in the whole body pointed at recovery, and nothing anywhere about help, undo, keyboard shortcuts or progressive disclosure. **No resolution is available** — the two bodies of work are not in conflict, they are aimed at different halves, and only one half has any measurement behind it.

**Put together, the thesis:** *the visible half of craft is cheap to check and largely already checkable; the half that measurably fails is functional and almost entirely unchecked; and the reflex of fixing a miss by adding a sentence is the weakest available move — sometimes weaker than doing nothing.* The practical consequence is an inversion of the obvious advice: when x is missed, the first question is not "what rule would have prevented this" but "**what program could have decided this, and why was it not running**" — and the second is "is this class functional or visual", because the corpus will help you with one and not the other.

## The hole

Docs 05 and 06 were researched in parallel by different agents, in different venues, and reached the same result:

- **No published measurement exists, anywhere, that a rules file or a review agent reduces the recurrence of a class of miss.** Doc 05 went looking on X and in the rules-file corpus and found none; doc 06 went looking on GitHub, in vendor docs, in HN/Reddit (blocked) and in the survey literature and found none. Doc 06 states it flatly: *"Everything in the loop is measured except the loop."*
- **No tooling turns a correction into a proposed rule.** A GitHub search for self-improving / retrospective rule generators returned **zero repositories**. The largest artifact in the category is a 40.8k-star library of hand-written rules by stack — a library, not a generator. Claude Code's auto-memory `feedback` type is the closest shipped thing and is machine-local, unversioned, and not guaranteed to capture any given correction.
- **No review agent is benchmarked on design defects.** Every published benchmark in the category counts logic bugs. Recall on "the empty state is missing" or "the primary action is not dominant" is unmeasured for every product.
- **No rubric library for design exists** in `claude plugin eval`, promptfoo, DeepEval or any commercial platform. Every design rubric is authored from scratch, every time, by every team.
- **The miss ledger has no product.** `/speckit.converge` writes deltas into *tasks*, `REVIEW.md` accumulates *rules*, auto-memory accumulates *notes* — three silos, no join, and the join is the thing that would answer "did it recur".
- And the community has been arguing about where the miss should land for **a year without resolution** — [spec-kit discussion #152](https://github.com/github/spec-kit/discussions/152), 44 comments and 88 replies, seven mutually exclusive positions, still the reference thread [fetched].

**What follows from this.** The stream's own premise — that a miss should become a durable line so its class stops recurring — is **unvalidated**. It is well-motivated: the mechanism evidence (V1–V3) is strong, the vendor's own docs argue for durable external artifacts over longer conversations, and the alternative (remember harder) is falsified. But motivation is not measurement, and the honest position is that this repo is proposing a practice, not reporting a validated one. The experiment roadmap exists because of this paragraph.

## Experiment roadmap — E0 to E7

Ordered cheapest-first, each with a kill criterion. E0 can end the programme for the cost of a notebook column.

**E0 — The recurrence baseline. One integer per miss, ten prototypes, $0.**
Before building any rubric, log every miss found at first human test with a class label from doc 01's twelve-class taxonomy and a `recurrence_key`. **The whole programme rests on one unmeasured assumption: that miss classes repeat across *independent* artifacts.** If they do not, promotion test 1 of 5 fails on every candidate, no rubric can ratchet, and the correct answer to the owner's question is "gates for the classes that recur inside a single build, and nothing else."
*Kill criterion:* under ~30% of misses fall into a class already seen in an earlier, unrelated prototype. *Closes:* doc 03 §2's open question — "No public data exists on the base rate of design-defect recurrence in AI-generated prototypes."

**E1 — Fix forward vs cleared-and-re-anchored. 20 defects, a weekend, tokens only.**
Take 20 real misses. Fix each twice: once in the polluted session, once after `/clear` with re-anchoring on the file, the spec and the defect report. Grade blind against the rubric. Doc 02 §2 is explicit that **no public measurement of this comparison exists**, and it is the question every practitioner answers by folklore. *Kill criterion for the restart doctrine:* fix-forward wins or ties. *Closes:* V3's falsifier and the strongest single piece of advice in doc 02.

**E2 — Measure `k`, the bloat parameter. ~$8, one afternoon.**
Generate the same prompt 10× against rubrics of n = 5, 15, 30, 60 criteria, each carrying a trivially verifiable canary criterion, and count criteria actually honored. Fit `a(n) = a₀ − k(n−1)`; the optimum is `n* = (a₀/k + 1)/2`. This reconciles the three rules of thumb in circulation — Anthropic's 200 lines (`k`≈0.002), the practitioner "~60 rules" (`k`≈0.008), and the instruction-stacking collapse at 24 (`k`≈0.020) — as **one model at three values of one parameter**, and replaces an argument with a number that is a property of how *your* criteria are written. Doc 07 §4.2 specifies it; the canary is also the only bloat detector a solo practitioner can actually run.

**E3 — Does the ratchet work? 3–6 weeks, two integers per prototype.**
The hole, closed at the cheapest honest resolution. A run chart of misses per prototype at first human test (median frozen from the first 12 points, annotated at every model/harness/rubric change) plus a running **repeat-offence rate** — what share of this week's misses were in classes that already had a criterion. The repeat-offence tally is a direct test of the loop's core claim and does not depend on the run chart at all. The stronger version, if you can afford it: give half the recurring classes a durable artifact and half nothing, and compare. *What it cannot prove:* causation, effect size, or transfer — it is a change-detector, not an experiment, and doc 07 §7.3 says so in those words.

**E4 — The Hodges experiment on design artifacts. 20 screens, 6 raters, two instruments.**
Give expert- and novice-produced (or restrained- and maximal-) UIs to raters with a binary checklist and with a global scale. Does the checklist invert the ranking, as it did in medicine? *If it does,* contradiction (a) is confirmed on this domain and the binary-for-regression / holistic-for-quality split becomes a hard rule rather than a synthesis. *If it does not,* the checklist can carry more of the rubric than doc 03 allows.

**E5 — Screenshot-loop and browser-agent recall on seeded misses. Cheap, does not exist.**
Seed N known design misses across the twelve classes, run the design-review agent / screenshot loop over them, count catches by class. Two separate docs name this independently as the highest-value missing measurement ([doc 01 §4](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md), [doc 06 §7](06-community-practice-and-tooling.md)). It is also the only way to know which rungs of the craft floor are earning their keep.

**E6 — The patch seam base rate.** What fraction of patches introduce a *neighbour disagreement* — a value that is on-scale but differs from every sibling — or increase the raw-value count? The delta checks are two lines each and already specified ([doc 04 §6](04-design-craft-as-checkable-criteria.md)); nobody has published the number. This is the measurement that tells you whether "patch" is as safe as this synthesis assumes in step 9.

**E7 — Does located evidence raise criterion agreement?** Score the same criteria twice — once with a free-text rationale, once requiring the rationale to cite a selector, bbox or quoted string. Measure κ. Doc 03 §4 calls this "a cheap, high-value experiment"; it is also [eval-tuning-loops/00](../eval-tuning-loops/00-synthesis.md)'s gap 2 (DOM-augmented judging) asked at the criterion level rather than the grade level, so one run serves two streams.

## Adoption order for a solo designer

Sequenced so each step is cheap, survives the next bad week, and earns the one after it. Steps 1–4 are an afternoon and free.

1. **Make the miss visible before you are the one who finds it.** Put the craft floor in CI — axe at AA, no horizontal scroll at 320/375/414/768/1280/1920, `:focus-visible` on every interactive element, no raw color or off-scale spacing outside the token layer, a placeholder grep, and an explicit loading/empty/error rendering for every data-touching component. Twenty checks, all deterministic, all in [doc 04 §C](04-design-craft-as-checkable-criteria.md). This is where V4 pays: eight of twelve miss classes have no better home.
2. **Adopt the patch protocol, especially its first four steps.** Freeze evidence, commit, scope-test, write the check — then choose the move. Package the [minimal-diff prompt](02-patch-regenerate-or-restart.md) as a `/repair` command so the four steps stop being optional. Adopt the two-correction stopping rule as an actual rule.
3. **Start the ledger before you start the rubric.** One row per miss: class, severity, `round_found`, what found it, **what should have found it**, and a `recurrence_key`. Six fields is the minimum viable version ([doc 01 §9](01-anatomy-of-a-miss.md)). This *is* E0, it costs seconds per miss, and without it every later decision is a guess. Add the 15-second Sign Out to the end of every session and hook-trigger it.
4. **Grade in a fresh session, artifact only.** Zero cost, one command, and it addresses the only bias in the stream with a measured effect size on this exact task shape. Blind-grade whenever you have a favourite.
5. **Write the first criterion only on the second occurrence — and write the gate first.** Run the five promotion tests. If a hook, lint rule, schema slot or DOM assertion can decide it, that is where it goes and the rubric gets nothing. What reaches the rubric should be atomic, binary, "Fails if…", and it must fail on the artifact that motivated it.
6. **Adopt the starter rubric, not a rubric of your own.** Six deterministic gates, five judged criteria, two human criteria ([doc 03 §Starter rubric](03-from-miss-to-rubric-line.md)). Never show more than seven judged lines per review event, randomise their order, and keep `h.ship` — *would you put this in front of a customer as-is?* — permanently, as the rubric-free tripwire that catches the rubric optimising itself.
7. **Write a `REVIEW.md` even if you never enable the managed service.** Its six levers — severity redefined *for a prototype repo*, a nit cap, a skip list for anything CI already enforces, repo-specific checks, a `file:line` verification bar, and a re-review convergence rule — are the best published review-rubric format and are copyable as a template. Note that the local `/code-review` does not read it.
8. **Add a screenshot baseline at three viewports**, fonts stabilised and animations frozen, committed to git. Component-scoped, not page-scoped: after a patch, the neighbours are what should not have moved.
9. **Measure, minimally, from week one.** Three integers and a grep per prototype. Twelve points takes 3–6 weeks at 2–4 prototypes a week. The repeat-offence rate is the falsification test for everything above.
10. **Then, and only then, buy anything.** A hosted visual-diff service when you outgrow the local gate (Argos first — it is the only maintained OSS-core option left). An eval case per recurring class, with the no-plugin control arm, when you are tuning a *generator* rather than an artifact. An LLM-judge criterion last: it needs ~4 recurrences a month before it is net positive, and its false-positive term dominates.

**And the anti-adoption rule, which matters as much:** do not run any of this on a genuinely disposable prototype. The unit of recurrence is the **class × generator**, not the artifact — so a throwaway carries the portfolio rubric (free, marginal cost zero) and **no prototype-specific criteria**, and the floor items people want to apply to it are mostly promotion-gate items that have been misfiled as rubric items ([doc 07 §9.3](07-loop-economics-and-ownership.md)). Moving them is free and shrinks the rubric, which improves the criteria that remain.
